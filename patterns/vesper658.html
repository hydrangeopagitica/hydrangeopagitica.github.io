Vesper, 6:58PM

Fair-faced Ariadne’s string trails behind
And my clockwork searches for a center.
My metal teeth, they wind and wind and wind (and wind)
and wind. There is an end to this I know.
The Sun falls; the world takes on a mass of shadow
And these halls of gray reflect a dying violet light.

I move like light moves through warped glass,
bending through the bulging stomachs of this structure,
through its negative space.
What is the shape of a labyrinth when you’re in it?
just another chamber
and another chamber

But at the end of a trail of four-faced rooms,
at the center of this hulking mass,
Pasiphae’s bastard son is nowhere to be found,
this I know.
The world tastes bitter on my tongue.

I am a mechanical insect tethered to a wall
which faces in on a shadow of a shadow of a
stomach’s surface.
What is the shape of a labyrinth when you’re in it?
(A rolled up ball of string that has forgotten
where it led.)

A warm breath from the wind whisks me away
from the wall and I almost fall off its
edge. It never lets me spy above the gray
But when it skims my body it tells me
where it is taking me. My second hand flies.
The world decompresses and I soar through its distances under the still-violet sky.

Olivia Schwartz

Summer 2024, Los Angeles
