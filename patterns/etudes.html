Two Etudes

I. Bronze Age Collapse

Hinterland frost paralyzes a hovel’s rot,
Sunders flowers and mold and eyes of men:
Penicillin syringes strewn with stoppers drawn
Waiting for a wild spring to thaw their spores.

Birch pulp sunlit through glacier-clear syringes,
Men with wild eyes strewn about the reeds.
The first bees thaw the flower’s spores
And functionaries enter hovels.

Thousand-stung pus mountains
Breach the functionary’s swollen
Face and the bees look clearly on
Their wild work under the birch.

Wild men reuse syringes
Leaning paralyzed against
The hovel’s beams seduced by ivy.
The ivy will be frozen out.


II. Diana's Foresters

We counted how many of their Staten Island lawns we could trample before the cops got wind under the crape curtain-sleeves of our chaste mistress the moon.

We courted winter in her shade. We sold 500 USD worth of information to Pennsylvanian fentanyl traffickers. We each bought a different digestif. We compared umbrellas. We didn’t sneak into the National Museum in Tokyo because Japanese security guards are too scrupulous.

We are governed as the sea is. We commandeered his living room when he was on vacation. We projected a movie on the wrong bedsheet. We borrowed someone’s uncle’s forklift. We tried to pull an all-nighter and awoke in each other’s arms, surrounded by empty boxes of knockoff Oreos, je vous aime je vous aime je vous aime. We swore ten thousand years of dark enmity against him and waged an unsuccessful smear campaign against his best friend. We edited his maps.

We turned a lithe eye around. We managed to get a spot in a ghost gun group buy (thanks to the Pennsylvanians) but we don’t really know what to do with them now except to occasionally turn to one another and say “I keep a strap on me.” We agreed it best to be lacking during our next noble galavant in Staten Island.

We started using the sewers.


Olivia Schwartz
